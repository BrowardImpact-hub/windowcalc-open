The Approval Flow

1
Quote drops below rep floor — system flags, proposal blocked
2
Rep submits approval request — logged with margin gap and note
3
Manager reviews — approve or deny with reason
4
If below manager floor — escalates to owner automatically
5
Decision recorded permanently — immutable, attributed, timestamped
Segregation of Duties

The rep who prices a job can never approve their own exception. This is a recognized internal control standard — hard-coded into the system.

For the CPA

Financial controls your auditor will recognize

WindowCalc is not just a quoting tool. It is a financial control system that generates proposals. Show this section to your accountant on day one.

⚖️

Gross Margin % — Not Markup

All reporting uses Gross Margin % — the correct P&L metric. Markup % is shown separately for those who need it. Your CPA will know the difference immediately.

🔒

Frozen Proposal Snapshots

Once a proposal is delivered, prices are locked in an immutable snapshot. WindowCalc cannot retroactively change what a customer was shown. Clean invoice reconciliation.

📜

Immutable Audit Trail

Every pricing change, approval, and user action is permanently logged with timestamp and user identity. Cannot be deleted. The data your auditor would ask for in a revenue control review.

🏗️

Revenue Recognition Stages

Quote statuses map to P&L stages: Draft (unbooked), Approved (committed — bookable when work begins), Completed (earned — invoice-ready). Structured for ASC 606.

🚧

Cost Visibility by Role

Sales reps never see cost data — enforced server-side. Owners and managers see full cost breakdown. This is role-based access control applied to financial data.

👥

Two-Person Authorization

No single employee controls pricing AND approves exceptions. This is the COSO-recognized two-person rule built as a hard constraint — not a policy that can be ignored.

CPA Summary

"Every pricing exception requires a named decision-maker with a timestamp. No job can be sold below margin floor without a named manager or owner creating an explicit approval record. This is a documented authorization trail that any auditor can follow from quote to decision to outcome."
